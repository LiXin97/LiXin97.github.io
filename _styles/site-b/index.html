Three questions about large language models. I treat them as one loop, not three separate areas.

  1. 01Measure

    Does a score mean what it appears to mean — and would we notice if it didn’t?

    4,027 problems from 836 arXiv papers, each shipping its own contamination evidence — WirelessMathBench-XL, NeurIPS 2026

  2. 02Train

    Can we reward the reasoning we are able to check, rather than the answer we hope for?

    +1.2–3.1 points over label routing, at 2B, 4B and 9B — domain-normalized multi-teacher distillation

  3. 03Compose

    When several models work together, does the interaction help — or quietly destroy answers that were already right?

    253 collapses in 6,925 logged debates — DebateLedger, NeurIPS 2026

Return leg

Composition creates new failure modes, which have to be measured too. That is what closes the loop.

Common ground across all three: domains where an answer can be checked — formal verification, mathematics, code, wireless systems.

  1. Measure NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track, poster First author of 7

    WirelessMathBench-XL

    An Auditable Benchmark for Wireless Mathematical Reasoning

    4,027 problems drawn from 836 arXiv papers across 20 wireless subfields. Every problem ships 13-gram contamination-overlap evidence against public pretraining text. Frontier models cluster at 86.5–91.3%.

    ScopeThat evidence is a rerunnable audit producing per-problem evidence for one lexical channel. It is not proof that the benchmark is clean, and the claim was deliberately narrowed to say so.

    Project page arXiv 2509.23219

  2. Compose NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track, poster Joint first author of 3

    Measuring Collapse and Correction in Homogeneous-Panel LLM Debate

    DebateLedger

    Across 6,925 logged MMLU-Pro debates, 253 collapses — a correct majority turned wrong. A probe-gated freeze prevents 29 of them but gives up 108 corrections: net −79 under equal weights. 58.9% of collapses begin in the first debate round.

    ScopeThe net of −79 is stated under equal weights for a prevented collapse and a lost correction. Both sides are logged on the same scale, which is what makes the trade visible at all.

    Project page

  3. Measure ICLR 2026 Joint first author of 5

    Local Success Does Not Compose

    Benchmarking Large Language Models for Compositional Formal Verification — DafnyComp

    A model can verify a function on its own and still fail once the specifications have to compose. The benchmark measures the distance between those two results.

    Project page OpenReview arXiv 2509.23061

  4. Train arXiv preprint First author of 9 2026

    Beyond Teacher Assignment

    Domain-Normalized Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation — DN-MOPD

    Label routing decides which teacher supervises a prompt, but not how strongly that teacher’s feedback counts. Normalizing the feedback scale per domain adds 1.2–3.1 points over label routing, across 2B, 4B and 9B students.

    Project page arXiv 2609.35347 Code Data Models

  5. Train Findings of EMNLP 2026 First author of 3

    Target-Local Verifier Choice in Best-of-K Reasoning Selection

    TLVC

    On a 34-generator, 7-verifier Best-of-K math panel, one strong process reward model is the best fixed verifier overall — and still not the best verifier for every generator. Label-free candidate statistics predict which verifier class wins.

    Code no preprint yet

    Benchmark SWE AI Coding for Ascend CANN

    400+ SWE-level task instances from real Ascend CANN repositories: multi-file, multi-language and execution-aware, on a live benchmarking paradigm that mitigates leakage.
