Research

Measure, train, compose

Three threads that I describe as one loop rather than three separate areas. The loop is the argument; the papers are the evidence.

Common ground

All three threads live in domains where an answer can be checked: formal verification, mathematics, code, wireless systems. That constraint is not incidental. It is what makes a reward signal honest, a benchmark auditable, and a disagreement between two models resolvable.

01 · Measure

Does a score mean what it appears to mean — and would we notice if it didn’t?

Benchmarks and evaluation protocols built to be audited rather than trusted: per-problem provenance, verifier-checkable answers, and an explicit limit on what a score supports.

A leaderboard number is a summary of a measurement procedure, and most of the interesting information is in the procedure. So I build benchmarks that ship their own evidence: where each problem came from, how its answer can be checked mechanically, and what the resulting number does and does not license you to say.

The last part is the one people skip. On WirelessMathBench-XL the contamination claim was deliberately narrowed during review, and the narrowed version is the one that ships: a rerunnable audit producing per-problem evidence for one lexical channel, not a proof that the benchmark is clean. A benchmark that overstates its own cleanliness is a measurement instrument with an uncalibrated offset.

  • NeurIPS 2026Evaluations and Datasets Track, posterFirst author of 7

    WirelessMathBench-XL — An Auditable Benchmark for Wireless Mathematical Reasoning

    4,027 problems from 836 arXiv papers across 20 subfields; 13-gram contamination-overlap evidence attached to every problem. Frontier models cluster at 86.5–91.3%.

    ScopeOne lexical channel, rerunnable, per problem. Not proof of a clean benchmark.

02 · Train

Can we reward the reasoning we are able to check, rather than the answer we hope for?

Reinforcement learning, on-policy distillation and verifier-guided selection.

Once you have an instrument that can be trusted about a narrow thing, you can train against it. The failure mode is subtle: a reward that correlates with the right answer is not the same as a reward for the reasoning, and the gap is where models learn to be convincing instead of correct.

Two findings from this thread sit together. In multi-teacher distillation, routing a prompt to the right teacher is only half the decision — how loudly that teacher is allowed to speak matters independently, and normalizing it per domain is worth 1.2–3.1 points. In Best-of-K selection, there is a single best fixed verifier, and it is still the wrong verifier for some generators. Both say the same thing: the supervision signal has structure that a single global setting throws away.

  • arXiv preprint 2026First author of 9

    Beyond Teacher Assignment — Domain-Normalized Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

    Label routing decides which teacher supervises a prompt, but not how strongly that teacher’s feedback counts. Normalizing the feedback scale per domain adds 1.2–3.1 points over label routing, across 2B, 4B and 9B students.

  • Findings of EMNLP 2026First author of 3

    Target-Local Verifier Choice in Best-of-K Reasoning Selection (TLVC)

    On a 34-generator, 7-verifier math panel, one strong process reward model is the best fixed verifier overall — and still not the best verifier for every generator. Label-free candidate statistics predict which verifier class wins.

  • TMLR 2026Joint first authorFifth in an equal-contribution block

    Re:Form — Reducing Human Priors in Scalable Formal Software Verification with RL in LLMs: A Preliminary Study on Dafny

    Reinforcement learning for formal software verification in Dafny, with the human priors reduced. The paper calls itself a preliminary study, and the title is the claim.

03 · Compose

When several models work together, does the interaction help — or quietly destroy answers that were already right?

Protocols, memory and measurement for systems where several models interact.

Multi-model systems are usually reported by their final accuracy, which hides the two things that actually happened: answers that were wrong and got fixed, and answers that were right and got broken. Counting only the first is how a harmful intervention gets published as a safe one.

So the unit of analysis is the transition, not the outcome. Across 6,925 logged debates there were 253 collapses, and a probe-gated freeze that prevents 29 of them gives up 108 corrections to do it. Under equal weights that is a net loss of 79. You only get to see that trade if both sides are logged on the same scale.

  • NeurIPS 2026Evaluations and Datasets Track, posterJoint first author of 3

    Measuring Collapse and Correction in Homogeneous-Panel LLM Debate (DebateLedger)

    253 collapses in 6,925 logged MMLU-Pro debates; a probe-gated freeze prevents 29 and gives up 108, net −79 under equal weights. 58.9% of collapses begin in the first round, which is also where an intervention has to act.

Before, and alongside

Wireless and robotics

Where the instinct for checkable domains came from — and why the wireless benchmarks exist at all.

I spent five years on systems that fail loudly: visual-inertial odometry, indoor mapping, robot localization. In those domains you cannot talk your way past an error — the robot drifts, or the map is wrong, and everybody can see it. That is the standard I want for a reasoning benchmark. Radio propagation and remote sensing came later, and brought the wireless benchmarks with them.

  • IEEE TSP 2026Trans. on Signal ProcessingSecond author of 4

    Stacked Intelligent Metasurface–Diffractive Deep Neural Networks — onboard terrain classification from SAR Level-0 raw data

    Roughly 90% accuracy classifying terrain directly from Level-0 raw SAR, with a stacked metasurface performing the inference in-wave — before digitisation or downlink. Extended from a workshop paper at ML4RS @ ICLR 2025.
