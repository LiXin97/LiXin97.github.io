I build measurements for language models that are meant to be audited rather than trusted — then train on the part that can be checked, and measure again once several models have to work together.

Three threads, which are really one loop: measure, train, compose. Each carries a question, and each returns to the first.

§ 01The loop

Not three areas. One circuit, walked repeatedly.

  1. 01

    Measure

    Does a score mean what it appears to mean, and would we notice if it didn’t?

    Benchmarks and evaluation protocols built to be audited rather than trusted: per-problem provenance, verifier-checkable answers, and an explicit limit on what a score supports.

  2. 02

    Train

    Can we reward the reasoning we are able to check, rather than the answer we hope for?

    Reinforcement learning, on-policy distillation and verifier-guided selection.

  3. 03

    Compose

    When several models work together, does the interaction help, or quietly destroy answers that were already right?

    Protocols, memory and measurement for systems where several models interact.

The return leg  ·  Composition creates new failure modes, which have to be measured too — so the third thread hands back to the first.

Common ground: domains where an answer can be checked — formal verification, mathematics, code, wireless systems. How the loop is actually walked →

Beyond Teacher AssignmentDomain-Normalized Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

Thread 03  Compose

Measuring Collapse and Correction in Homogeneous-Panel LLM DebateDebateLedger

Across 6,925 logged MMLU-Pro debates, 253 collapses — a correct majority talked into a wrong one. A probe-gated freeze prevents 29 of those collapses but gives up 108 corrections: net −79. And 58.9% of collapses begin in the first debate round.

Scope  The net figure is under equal weights for collapses and corrections.

§ 03Also on the record

Formal verification is the other place where answers are checkable, and the same composition problem shows up there. DafnyComp (ICLR 2026, joint first author) finds that a model can verify a function on its own and still fail once the specifications have to compose; the benchmark measures the distance between those two results. Re:Form (TMLR 2026, joint first author) studies how far RL can push Dafny verification with fewer human priors.

Before the LLM work: wireless systems, radio maps and robot perception — including a localisation stack that runs on a fleet of 1,000+ cleaning robots. It is not the headline, but it is where the habit of working only in checkable domains comes from. Read that part →
