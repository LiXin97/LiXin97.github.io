The loop

Three verbs rather than three fields. Each carries a question; each hands its answer, and its new failure modes, to the next.

The common ground is narrow on purpose: domains where an answer can be checked — formal verification, mathematics, code, wireless systems. If a claim cannot be checked, it is not a place where any of this work has anything to say.

§ 01Measure

Benchmarks and evaluation protocols.

Does a score mean what it appears to mean, and would we notice if it didn’t?

The working answer is to build benchmarks that can be audited rather than trusted: per-problem provenance, verifier-checkable answers, and an explicit limit on what a score supports. The limit is part of the artefact — if it has to be narrowed during review, the narrowing is published with it.

4,027 problems drawn from 836 arXiv papers across 20 wireless subfields; every problem ships 13-gram contamination-overlap evidence against public pretraining text. Frontier models cluster at 86.5–91.3%.

§ 02Train

RL, on-policy distillation, verifier-guided selection.

Can we reward the reasoning we are able to check, rather than the answer we hope for?

Once a domain is checkable, the checker becomes a training signal — and then the question is how much to trust that signal, from which teacher, for which prompt.

arXiv preprint 2026First of 9 authors

DN-MOPD — Beyond Teacher Assignment

Label routing decides which teacher supervises a prompt but not how strongly that teacher’s feedback counts. Normalising the feedback scale per domain adds 1.2–3.1 points over label routing, across 2B, 4B and 9B students.

§ 03Compose

Protocols, memory, and measurement for many-model systems.

When several models work together, does the interaction help, or quietly destroy answers that were already right?

Composition is usually measured by final accuracy, which hides the trade: the same mechanism that rescues wrong answers also talks correct ones out of being correct. Counting both directions is the whole point.

NeurIPS 2026Eval & Datasets · posterJoint first of 3 authors

DebateLedger — Measuring Collapse and Correction in Homogeneous-Panel LLM Debate

Across 6,925 logged MMLU-Pro debates, 253 collapses: a correct majority turned wrong. A probe-gated freeze prevents 29 collapses but gives up 108 corrections — net −79 under equal weights. 58.9% of collapses begin in the first debate round.

§ 04Before

The wireless and robotics years, 2018–2025.

The habit of working only where an answer can be checked did not come from language models. It came from years of systems that fail loudly: a robot that mislocalises walks into a wall, and a radio map that is wrong is wrong at a measurable number of decibels. That is the standard the LLM work is trying to hold onto.

Roughly 90% accuracy classifying terrain directly from Level-0 raw SAR, with a stacked metasurface performing the inference in-wave — before digitisation or downlink. Extended from a workshop paper at ML4RS @ ICLR 2025.
