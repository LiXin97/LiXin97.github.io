Xin Li李鑫

I build measurements that decide whether a reasoning score can be believed, and training methods whose reward can be checked. WirelessMathBench was the first benchmark for LLM mathematical reasoning in wireless communications; its successor WirelessMathBench-XL ships a rerunnable contamination audit, so every problem carries its own lexical-overlap evidence against public pretraining text. DebateLedger separates the harmful collapses in multi-agent debate from the useful corrections, and shows that an intervention judged only on collapse prevention can be net negative once the corrections it gives up are counted. DN-MOPD makes the scale of each teacher's feedback, not just the choice of teacher, the thing to get right in multi-teacher on-policy distillation; the checkpoints and data are released.

The three threads are one loop. Measure whether a score means what it appears to mean; train against rewards that can be checked instead of hoped for; then compose several models and measure what the composition quietly breaks. Mostly in domains where an answer can be verified — formal verification, mathematics, code, and wireless systems.

Ph.D. student at Nanyang Technological University, Singapore, advised by Prof. Chau Yuen. Previously robot perception at Gausium Robotics, Microsoft Research Asia and MEGVII.

NeurIPS 2026  ·  Xin Li, Mengbing Liu, Yiyang Zhu, Wenhe Zhang, Li Wei, Jiancheng An, Chau Yuen

4,027 problems drawn from 836 arXiv papers across 20 wireless subfields, each shipped with 13-gram contamination-overlap evidence against public pretraining text — a rerunnable audit of one lexical channel, not proof that the set is clean. Frontier models cluster at 86.5–91.3%.

Measuring Collapse and Correction in Homogeneous-Panel LLM Debate

NeurIPS 2026  ·  Xin Li*, Mengbing Liu*, Chau Yuen

Across 6,925 logged MMLU-Pro debates, 253 turned a correct majority wrong and 58.9% of those collapses begin in the first round; a probe-gated freeze prevents 29 of them but gives up 108 corrections — net −79 under equal weights.

Background

Before the Ph.D. I worked on robot perception: multimodal localization at Gausium Robotics, where I led a team of five engineers building a vision, LiDAR and Wi-Fi stack that reached 95% localization accuracy across 100,000+ m² and is deployed on a fleet of 1,000+ commercial cleaning robots; RGB-D and IMU indoor mapping at Microsoft Research Asia; and visual-inertial odometry at MEGVII. I was a research assistant at NTU from 2024 until the Ph.D. began in 2025, and hold an M.E. from Peking University and a B.E. from Northeastern University, China.

Funded by the NTU Research Scholarship, with research grants from a Google Gemini Academic Program Award, the Modal Academics Compute Grant, a Cohere Labs Catalyst Grant and the OpenAI Researcher Access Program. Rohde & Schwarz Award at the IEEE 6G Summit Singapore and PREMIA Best Student Paper Award finalist, both 2025. I have supervised three dissertation and final-year-project students at NTU and NUS, co-organized AIR4D at IROS 2025, and review for NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, AAAI, CVPR, ECCV, AISTATS, SIGGRAPH, IROS, ICRA, IEEE RA-L, ACM TOG and IEEE TNNLS.
