Measure

Built to be audited, not trusted

The question

Does a score mean what it appears to mean, and would we notice if it didn’t?

A benchmark is an instrument. An instrument you cannot open is a rumour with a decimal point. Three things make one auditable, and none of them is a number.

  1. Provenance, per problem. Not “sourced from the literature” — this problem, from this paper.
  2. Answers a verifier can check. Grading that does not depend on a judge’s taste, which is why the domains are proofs, mathematics, code and wireless modelling.
  3. A stated limit. One sentence on what the score does not support.

The first two are work. The third is a decision, and it is the one that gets dropped, because it is the only one that costs you a claim. Below, the limit is printed next to the result it belongs to — including the one that was deliberately narrowed during review.

Ordered by what each one shows, not by date. Dates, venues and full author lists are on the Record.

  1. NeurIPS 2026
    Evaluations & Datasets
    (poster) 1st of 7

    4,027 problems drawn from 836 arXiv papers across 20 wireless subfields. Every problem ships 13‑gram contamination‑overlap evidence against public pretraining text, so the audit can be rerun by someone who does not trust me. Frontier models cluster at 86.5–91.3%.

    WirelessMathBench‑XL: An Auditable Benchmark for Wireless Mathematical Reasoning

    Xin Li, Mengbing Liu, Yiyang Zhu, Wenhe Zhang, Li Wei, Jiancheng An, Chau Yuen

    • Project
    • arXiv:2509.23219

    What this does not show

    During review the contamination claim was deliberately narrowed, and the narrow version is the true one. What ships is a rerunnable audit producing per‑problem evidence for one lexical channel. It is not proof that the benchmark is clean.

    A 13‑gram overlap test sees verbatim lexical reuse. It does not see paraphrase, translation, or a model that learned the physics from somewhere else. The honest reading of the audit is “no evidence of lexical leakage on this one channel”, and nothing wider than that.
