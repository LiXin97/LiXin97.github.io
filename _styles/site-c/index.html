00The diagram

One loop, three legs

Most of this work gets filed as three separate things — evaluation, post‑training, agents. Filed that way it reads as a person with three hobbies. Read as a cycle it reads as one position: you are only allowed to claim what you can check, and composing systems keeps producing things nobody can check yet.

The ground

All three legs stand on the same ground — formal verification, mathematics, code, wireless systems — domains where a claim can be settled by something other than a preference.

That constraint is not a limitation of the method. It is the method: it is what makes an audit rerunnable, a reward trustworthy, and a collapse countable.

Measure

Does a score mean what it appears to mean, and would we notice if it didn’t?

A benchmark is an instrument, and an instrument you cannot open is a rumour. So the ones I build carry their own provenance: where each problem came from, an answer a verifier can check, and a written limit on what the number supports. The third is the expensive one. It is where you have to state what your own result does not prove.

WirelessMathBench-XL · NeurIPS 2026 D&B

4,027 problems drawn from 836 arXiv papers across 20 wireless subfields, each one shipping 13‑gram contamination‑overlap evidence against public pretraining text. Frontier models cluster at 86.5–91.3%.

Train

Can we reward the reasoning we are able to check, rather than the answer we hope for?

Reward the final answer and you are training a model to guess well. Reward only what a verifier confirms and the work moves to where checking is cheap: proofs, mathematics, code. Two things then turn out to matter more than expected — how strongly a teacher’s feedback is allowed to count, and which verifier you trusted to pick the winning candidate.

DN‑MOPD · arXiv 2026

Label routing decides which teacher supervises a prompt, but not how strongly that teacher’s feedback counts. Normalising the feedback scale per domain adds 1.2–3.1 points over label routing, across 2B, 4B and 9B students.

Compose

When several models work together, does the interaction help, or quietly destroy answers that were already right?

Put several models in a room and the interaction becomes a variable nobody measured. It can repair a wrong answer. It can also take a correct majority and talk it out of being right. Protocols, memory and logging are what make the difference observable at all — and the moment it is observable, this leg hands the problem back to the first one.

DebateLedger · NeurIPS 2026 D&B

Across 6,925 logged MMLU‑Pro debates: 253 collapses, where a correct majority turned wrong. A probe‑gated freeze prevents 29 of them and gives up 108 corrections — net −79 under equal weights.

↵The return leg

Why it closes

Composition does not only add capability. It adds failure modes — and a failure mode nobody has an instrument for is indistinguishable from good news. So whatever the third leg produces becomes the next thing the first leg has to be able to see.

Two results sit exactly on that seam. DafnyComp asks a model to verify a function on its own, then asks it to make the specifications compose, and measures the distance between those two results; the title is the finding, local success does not compose. DebateLedger logs 6,925 debates and finds that 58.9% of collapses begin in the very first round — which is only a fact about debate because somebody kept the ledger.

Neither number existed before the instrument did. That is the argument this whole site is making, and it is why there are three pages instead of one list.

The systems years

The earlier record is robot perception and wireless systems: visual‑inertial odometry, planar regularities in SLAM, indoor radio‑map prediction, and terrain classification performed in‑wave by a stacked metasurface before the signal is ever digitised. At Gausium Robotics I led five engineers on hierarchical vision / LiDAR / Wi‑Fi localization — 95% accuracy across 100,000+ m², running on a fleet of 1,000+ active robots.

It is not in the loop and it is not hidden either. It is the reason the loop insists on checkable domains in the first place: a localization stack that is 95% right is a thing the floor will argue with you about.
