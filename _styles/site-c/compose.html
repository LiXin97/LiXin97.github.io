Compose

Interaction is a variable, not a feature

The question

When several models work together, does the interaction help, or quietly destroy answers that were already right?

Several models arguing is not a bigger model. It is a system with a new free variable — who speaks, what they remember, what counts as agreement — and that variable has its own failure modes, none of which show up in a component test. The aggregate win rate hides both directions at once: corrections and collapses cancel in the average, so a method can look neutral while doing a great deal of damage and a great deal of good.

The only way I have found to see it is to keep the ledger: log every round, attribute every majority flip, and then report the trade rather than the net. Measurement is not a postscript to composition. It is the part that makes composition a claim instead of a vibe.

    Read the trade, not the net

    The −79 is the answer under equal weights, and equal weights are an assumption, not a finding. If a wrong answer that survives review costs more than a right answer that is never reached, the same ledger supports the opposite decision. Publishing the two numbers separately is the point: 29 and 108 are measurements, the net is a policy.

↵The return leg

The loop closes here

None of the numbers on this page were available from the outside. An aggregate accuracy figure can be reported long before anyone can say how many correct answers were destroyed along the way, because the quantity that matters — a correct majority going wrong — has no instrument until somebody builds one. Composition produced the failure mode; the measurement had to be built afterwards to see it.

That is the return leg, and it is not a tidy metaphor. It is the reason DafnyComp exists: a model verifying one function at a time looks fine, and only a benchmark built specifically for composition can show the distance between local success and compositional failure. Every system I compose hands the first leg a new thing to measure.
