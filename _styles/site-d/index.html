Three threads that are really one loop: measure what a model does, train on the part you can actually check, then compose several models — and measure what that breaks.

Advised by Prof. Chau Yuen (IEEE Fellow), 2025–2029 (expected). Before the Ph.D.: robot perception and localisation, in research labs and on a shipping product.

The loop

  1. 01Measure

    Benchmarks and evaluation protocols built to be audited rather than trusted: per-problem provenance, verifier-checkable answers, and an explicit limit on what a score supports.

    Does a score mean what it appears to mean, and would we notice if it didn't?

  2. 02Train

    Reinforcement learning, on-policy distillation and verifier-guided selection.

    Can we reward the reasoning we are able to check, rather than the answer we hope for?

  3. 03Compose

    Protocols, memory and measurement for systems where several models interact.

    When several models work together, does the interaction help, or quietly destroy answers that were already right?

↻ back to 01 — composition creates new failure modes, which have to be measured too.

Common ground — domains where an answer can be checked: formal verification, mathematics, code, wireless systems.

Four results, and what each one covers

A number is only as good as the sentence that says what it covers. That sentence is the part that is easy to leave off, so here it is first.

  • WirelessMathBench-XL

    NeurIPS 2026
    Evaluations & Datasets    first of 7

    4,027 problems drawn from 836 arXiv papers across 20 wireless subfields. Every problem ships 13-gram contamination-overlap evidence against public pretraining text. Frontier models cluster at 86.5–91.3%.

    The limit, statedThe contamination check is a rerunnable audit producing per-problem evidence for one lexical channel. It is not proof that the benchmark is clean. The claim is deliberately narrow, and it stays narrow here.

  • DebateLedger

    NeurIPS 2026
    Evaluations & Datasets    joint first of 3

    Across 6,925 logged MMLU-Pro debates, 253 collapses — a correct majority turned wrong. A probe-gated freeze prevents 29 of them and gives up 108 corrections.

    ScopeThat is net −79 under equal weights. 58.9% of collapses begin in the first debate round.

  • DN-MOPD

    arXiv preprint 2026first of 9

    Label routing decides which teacher supervises a prompt, but not how strongly that teacher's feedback counts. Normalising the feedback scale per domain adds 1.2–3.1 points.

    ScopeOver label routing specifically, across 2B, 4B and 9B students. Multi-teacher on-policy distillation, not a claim about distillation in general.

  • TLVC

    Findings of EMNLP 2026first of 3

    On a 34-generator, 7-verifier Best-of-K math panel, one strong process reward model is the best fixed verifier overall.

    ScopeAnd still not the best verifier for every generator. Which verifier class wins is predictable from label-free candidate statistics.
